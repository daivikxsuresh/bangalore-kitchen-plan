4Reservations — ResyTerm locked · Aug 16

This is the only piece of the plan with a real monthly cost attached, so it gets its own section. We compared both platforms properly before choosing. Here's the whole case, in the open.

Locked Aug 16: Resy, "Platform" plan — $249/month, twelve-month term

$249/month, flat, with zero per-cover fees — and no setup fee. Billing does not start when you sign; it starts the day you go live. Table management, reservations, walk-in waitlists, automated guest text reminders and optional card-holds for no-show protection are all included at this tier. It goes on your account, in your business's name — you own it, I run the day-to-day.

Why twelve months and not three years. Resy priced the term three ways: 12 months at $249, 24 at $229, 36 at $189. The three-year deal saves roughly $60/month — about $2,200 over three years, and I still said no. The reason is simple: they have not yet put in writing what it costs you to get out early. A three-year contract with an unreadable exit is a liability on a business that's still growing, and it would outlast this plan by two years. Twelve months at $249 is us paying a small premium for the right to walk. I've asked for the early-termination and auto-renew terms anyway, in writing, and I'll put the answer here when it lands.

Correcting something I told you earlier, because you should hear it from me: I said renewal in year two would hold at $249. That was wrong. Resy has since confirmed they do not grandfather renewals — and their list price for new accounts has already moved from $249 to $289. So year two gets renegotiated, not inherited. That's not a reason to avoid the deal; it's a reason to diary it. I'll open the renewal conversation about ninety days before the anniversary, with a year of your own booking data in hand — which is a much stronger position than we're negotiating from today.

The one date that's actually time-sensitive: to hold $249 rather than $289, the contract must be signed and billing details entered by Wednesday, Sept 10. That's worth about $480 over the first year. Signing is not opening — billing still starts only on go-live, and the launch date can be set after signing without changing the price. So please don't let picking a launch date hold up the signature.

Sunita's question: "does this change our card fees?"

Short answer — no. Your POS and your regular checks are untouched.

You asked what the 2.9% + 30¢ applies to, and it's a good question because the answer decides whether this costs you anything beyond the subscription. It doesn't.

That rate only applies to cards Resy itself processes, which is exactly three situations: a deposit on a large party, a no-show hold if we choose to switch those on, and tickets sold for an event like Bollywood Night. Those are all cases where money moves before the guest ever sits down — so there's no POS involved to run it through.

A normal dinner check is not affected. The guest books on Resy, eats, and pays on your Clover exactly as they do today, at whatever rate you already pay. Resy never sees that transaction and never takes a cut of it. Confirmed in writing, Aug 16.

Resy's other tiers: Platform 360 at $399 adds deeper guest analytics. I'd skip it — those reports are only useful once there's a guest history to read, and on day one there isn't one. We can move up mid-contract if it ever earns its keep. Good news on Bollywood Night: I asked, and ticketed events are included on Platform at no extra monthly fee — only standard card processing (2.9% + 30¢) on tickets sold. I'd budgeted for a separate $269/month plan for this. We don't need one.

Put simply: OpenTable charges you more the busier you get. Resy doesn't. You're already packed — on a per-cover model you'd start paying a fee on guests who were walking through that door anyway. At a thousand network covers a month that's over $1,000 on top of the subscription. Resy is $249 whether you seat fifty people or five thousand.

Your Clover POS — I asked, and here's the honest answer

You're keeping Clover. Nothing about that changes.

Resy does not connect to Clover. Their two POS integrations are Toast and Square. I want you to hear that from me rather than discover it in month two.

Here's what it actually costs us, which is very little. A POS connection does one useful thing: it tells Resy a table has cleared the moment the check is closed. Without it, the host taps a button when a table gets cleared — that's the whole difference. Everything that made Resy the right call still works exactly the same: the bookings, the walk-in waitlist, the guest reminders, the Google listing, the flat $249.

Resy's own team told me under a third of their restaurants use a POS integration at all. I asked them directly whether we should switch systems to get it. The answer was no — and I agree. Migrating a POS in a kitchen that is full every service is a real risk for a convenience. We are not touching Clover. What I'll do instead is write up a one-page host-stand routine so every shift handles it the same way.
